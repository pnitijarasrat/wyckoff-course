Effort vs result: what is volume telling you?

Lesson 2 read a trend from price alone. Now you add volume, the second half of every Wyckoff read.

Your win today

Point at any move on a chart (a bar, a swing, or a breakout) and say whether volume and price are in harmony or divergence, and what that predicts.

The law in one line

“In financial markets, effort is represented by volume while the result is represented by price. … The aim is to assess the dominance of buyers or sellers through convergence and divergence between price and volume.”[Classic p.61]

Volume is effort because “the participation of large traders is identified by an increase in volume”[p.61]. If big money pushes hard, price should move a lot. When it doesn’t, someone on the other side is soaking up the push.

What it looks likeWhat it suggests
HarmonyEffort and result agree: big volume brings a big move, small volume a small oneStrength. The move is likely to continue
DivergenceEffort and result disagree: big volume brings a small move, or a big move comes on little volumeWeakness. A turn may be coming[p.62]

Size matters in both directions. “The price movement will be in direct proportion to the amount of effort expended,” and “a larger divergence, a larger result”[p.62].

Same rule as Lesson 2

Volume is always relative. “High” means high compared with this stock’s recent bars and swings, not a fixed number of shares. “Sudden relative increases or decreases in volume are significant.”[p.70]

Five places to read it

The book applies the law at increasing zoom levels[pp.64–70]. These are the five you’ll use most:

ScaleHarmonyDivergence
1 · One bar[p.65]Wide range on high volume; narrow range on low volumeThe reverse: narrow range on high volume (absorption) or wide range on low volume
2 · The bars after[p.65]The high-volume bar is followed by continuationThe high-volume bar is followed by a turn. One bar alone is not a verdict; the next bars decide
3 · Swings[p.66]Impulses on rising volume, pullbacks on falling volumeA new high (or low) on less volume, or a pullback on more volume
4 · Key levels[p.68]Volume breaks the level, and the break holdsVolume breaks the level, but price falls back: “all of that operated volume has been participating in the opposite direction”
5 · End of a trend[pp.69–70]Climax: very heavy volume with small progress after a long trend means the trend is nearly done. Lack of interest: very light volume at lows after a big fall means sellers have dried up (and the mirror at highs means buyers have)

Scale 3, up close: compare whole waves

David Weis’s idea, which the book adopts, is to add up all the volume in each swing and compare the totals wave against wave[pp.66–67]. The labels in the volume pane below do that. Harmony in an uptrend means the up-waves outweigh the down-waves, and each new high comes on a bigger up-wave.

Connecting to Lesson 2

In Lesson 2, a shrinking impulse was a warning. Volume tells you which kind of warning it is[2.0 p.36]:

  • A shorter push on heavy volume means “the big effort got little reward”. The other side is stepping in, so in an uptrend, supply is appearing.
  • A shorter push on light volume means exhaustion. The trend side is withdrawing, so in an uptrend, buyers are leaving.

The effort-vs-result read

  1. Find the stand-out volume: the biggest bars and the biggest waves on the visible chart.
  2. Check the result: how far did price move on that effort, and where did the bar close?
  3. Check what happened next: did the move continue or reverse? Did the break hold?
  4. Verdict: “harmony, so expect continuation” or “divergence, because …, so watch for a turn”. Like a broken demand line, divergence is an alert, not a trade on its own.

Real charts · Do it in Deepvue (~25 min)

  1. Take the same stocks you used for Lesson 2. If you skipped that homework, use any three daily uptrends and one daily downtrend.
  2. Swings: for the last two impulses and the pullback between them, eyeball the volume bars. Is the impulse volume rising or falling? Is the pullback volume lighter than the impulses?
  3. Stand-out bar: find the highest-volume day of the last 3 months (skip earnings days, or note them separately). Was the bar wide or narrow, and where did it close? Over the next 5 days, did price continue or turn?
  4. Levels: if the stock recently broke a support, resistance or trend line, did it break on volume, and did the break hold?

Primary source: read this next (~15 min)

Villahermosa, The Wyckoff Methodology in Depth (your Classic Wyckoff.pdf). Read Ch. 9 “The Law of Effort and Result” (PDF pp.61–70). The harmony/divergence table on p.63 is an image, so look at it in the PDF. It’s the best one-page summary of this lesson.

Second book: Villahermosa, Wyckoff 2.0, PDF p.36 (shortening thrust with heavy vs. light volume) and pp.84–86 (Weis waves applied to effort and result). Cross-check: Bogomazov & Lipsett, “The Wyckoff Method: A Tutorial” (StockCharts ChartSchool). Its AAPL example walks through three reactions: harmony, then reduced supply, then “the Effort increases while the Result decreases, showing the presence of large buyers absorbing supply.”
