Where is this stock in the cycle?

Before any Wyckoff label, entry or stop, answer one question: is this stock trending or ranging, and what came before?

Your win today

Open any daily chart and, within ten seconds, classify the right edge as one of four conditions. For a range, name the two structures it could turn into. Every later lesson builds on this call.

Why this comes first

Villahermosa puts it bluntly: knowing the general context “helps us avoid entering the wrong side of the market”. If a stock is in an uptrend after accumulation you avoid shorting it. If it is in a downtrend after distribution you avoid buying it. “You may not know how to take advantage of the trend movement; but with this premise in mind, you will surely avoid having a loss.”[Classic p.15]

For a long-only stock trader this is the first filter: don’t buy in a markdown.

Only two things can be happening

The market can only be in two kinds of condition[Classic p.14]:

ConditionObjective definitionWhat it means
UptrendRising highs and rising lowsDemand is more aggressive than supply
DowntrendFalling highs and falling lowsSupply is more aggressive than demand
Trading rangeHighs and lows keep returning to the same price bandRelative balance. A cause is being built[p.33]

Definitions from Classic pp.17–18. A single pullback inside a trend doesn’t make a range. The structure of highs and lows decides.

The cycle: four stages, two pauses

Put trends and ranges in sequence and you get Wyckoff’s price cycle. Bruce Fraser calls it “the classic cycle in financial asset prices… endlessly repeating”[Fraser].

StageTypeComes afterLeads toWho is doing what
AccumulationRangea falla riseProfessionals absorb the stock weak hands are selling
MarkupTrend ↑accumulationdistributionThe public joins and demand drives price up
DistributionRangea risea fallProfessionals sell to the last greedy buyers
MarkdownTrend ↓distributionaccumulationSupply dominates and fear drives price down
Re‑accumulationRangea risea further riseA pause where demand is refreshed mid-uptrend
RedistributionRangea falla further fallA pause where supply is refreshed mid-downtrend

Classic pp.14–16, 77, 86. Fraser describes re-accumulation and redistribution as pauses within markup and markdown.

The catch: a range only gets its name after it ends

Look at the table again. A range that follows a fall could be accumulation (then a rise) or redistribution (then a further fall). A range that follows a rise could be distribution or re‑accumulation.

Villahermosa: “until we visualize the effect of a cause we cannot determine what it is (accumulation or distribution)”[2.0 p.24]. He calls telling them apart “one of the most difficult tasks for the Wyckoff trader”[Classic p.87]. Most of this course teaches the clues that tilt the odds. Today you only learn to name both possibilities and stop calling a range something it hasn’t proven yet.

Two more facts to keep
  • Not every range is a campaign. Some are just a stock fluctuating with no professional interest. Ignore those[Classic p.34].
  • Every timeframe has its own cycle. The weekly chart can be in markup while the daily is in a range[Classic p.16]. Stay on the daily for now.

The ten-second read

  1. Find the last 3–4 swing highs and lows. Are both rising, both falling, or overlapping in a band?
  2. If it’s a trend, name it (markup or markdown). One pullback doesn’t break it. A lower low in an uptrend (or a higher high in a downtrend) does.
  3. If it’s a range, look left. What trend led into it? Write down the two structures it could become.

Drill 1 · Classify the right edge

Each chart stops at the dashed now line. Use only the bars to its left. All eight questions have the same four options. After you answer, the faded bars show what happened next.

Drill 2 · Name both possibilities

No charts this time. Answer from the logic of the cycle.

Drill 3 · Recall without looking

Scroll up so the tables are off-screen, then type your answer. Struggling to recall is what makes it stick.

Real charts · Do it in Deepvue (~25 min)

Synthetic charts are clean. Real ones aren’t. This is where the skill gets built.

  1. Open 10 stocks from your watchlist on the daily chart, zoomed to about 12–18 months.
  2. For each one, call the right edge: uptrend, downtrend, range after a rise or range after a fall.
  3. If it’s a range, draw a rectangle around it and write the two structures it could become.
  4. Bonus: flip to the weekly. Does it say the same thing? Note any that disagree.
  5. Paste your calls back to me in this format, and I’ll check them against the price data: 
    NVDA — uptrend — HH/HL since Aug, last pullback held above prior low
XYZ  — range after a fall — could be accumulation or redistribution
