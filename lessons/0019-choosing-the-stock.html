Which stock? From Wyckoff’s five steps to a scan

Eighteen lessons taught you to read one chart. But which chart? Wyckoff answered that before anything else: the market first, then the stocks in harmony with it, then the ones that have built a cause and are ready to move. This lesson turns his five steps into a scan you can run in Deepvue.

Your win today

Turn Wyckoff’s five steps into a two-stage routine: a scan that finds candidates, then a read that only your eye can do. Along the way, rank stocks by their RS line, measured from the right starting point.

Why this matters to you

Your mission starts with “given any US stock chart”. In practice, nobody hands you the chart. You pick it out of thousands. Villahermosa’s books never face this, because their examples are a handful of futures markets. Wyckoff traded stocks, and his method begins with selection. The StockCharts tutorial by Wyckoff Analytics’ founders lays it out as five steps[ChartSchool].

1 · The five steps

StepWyckoff’s questionWhat you look atIn this course
1Where is the market, and where is it probably going?The index: weekly, then dailyLessons 1, 10
2Which stocks are in harmony with it?Each stock against the index: the RS linetoday
3Which have a cause big enough for my target?A range: accumulation or re-accumulationLessons 7, 8
4Which are ready to move?The nine buying tests: Phase C done, or Phase D onLessons 5, 6, 18
5When?A turn in the index, then a stopLessons 8, 15

Steps 1 and 5 are about the market. Steps 2–4 are about the stock. The tutorial puts Step 2 bluntly: “In an uptrend, select stocks that are stronger than the market. In a downtrend, do the reverse”. It says to drop any stock you’re unsure about. Step 5 explains why the market comes first and last: “Three-quarters or more of individual issues move in sync with the overall market”[ChartSchool, “Wyckoff’s five-step approach”].

2 · The RS line: one ratio

RS line = the stock’s close ÷ the index’s close, drawn as a line. When it rises, the stock is beating the market, whatever its own price did. A stock that falls 10% while the index falls 15% has a rising RS line. Bruce Fraser uses the S&P 500 as the index “as it is the benchmark that many institutional managers are measured against”[Fraser 2017].

Three ideas from Fraser’s StockCharts columns:

  • In gear. Price above a rising 39-week average (about 200 days) and the RS line above its own 39-week average. “Persistence of the uptrend … is more likely when these ratios are InGear together”[Fraser 2018].
  • Top-down. Sector, then industry group, then stock: “Always seeking the strongest stocks in the strongest groups.” Early leaders are usually large caps. Institutions buy those first[Fraser 2017].
  • Inside a range. His COP example: the RS line turned up from the ST to the SOS, then held above its average on the pullback to the LPS[Fraser 2018]. So the RS line is one more trace to weigh in Phases B–D, alongside volume.

3 · Measure from the turn

There is a trap in Step 2. Stronger than the market, measured over which period? I checked one real episode: the spring 2025 selloff and recovery, across 45 large caps (the names from Lessons 16–17). Each was ranked by its RS line over two different periods, and then I looked at how each third did over the next four months.

RS ranked over…Top third, next 4 monthsBottom thirdIndex
The fall: index high → lowest close+4% (median)+37%+13%
The first 7 weeks off the low+36%+3%+13%

Forward window: end of May to end of September 2025. Daily closes, Nasdaq.com data.

The stocks that held up best during the fall were mostly defensive names, where money hides in a selloff. Once the market turned, they lagged. The leaders of the new markup showed themselves in the first weeks off the low. Part of that is plain volatility: the most volatile stocks bounce hardest. That still leaves you with leaders, but they swing more, so size by the stop (Lesson 8).

This is one episode, one group of big names I picked, and my own count: an illustration, not a backtest. What it supports is a working rule (mine), and it matches Wyckoff’s order of steps. Rank RS only after Step 1 says the market has turned, and measure it from the turn. During a decline, an RS leader is a name to watch, not a buy.

4 · From steps to a scan

A scan is a coarse, cheap filter. It handles Step 2 well and Step 3 roughly. It can’t do Step 4 at all: no filter can tell a Spring from an MSOW, as Lesson 18 showed. So the routine has two stages:

1 · The marketyour weekly read of the index. Longs only if it’s in markup or turning up
→
2 · The scanfilters for Steps 2–3. About 30–80 names
→
3 · The readweekly glance → 5–10 names. Full daily read → 1–3 plans
→
4 · The timingan alert at the trigger. Enter with the index, stop placed

A starting scan (my recipe. Adjust it once you see what it returns):

FilterStepWhy
Price ≥ $10, average dollar volume ≥ $20M a day—Your stops fill near their price (Lessons 14, 16). Thin stocks gap through them
Price above a rising 200-day average2The price half of Fraser’s “in gear”
Relative strength in the top 20–30%2Stronger than the market. Re-rank after an index turn
Within about 25% of its 52-week high3In a range near the top, not in markdown
Volatility contracting (ATR falling)3“Volatility diminishes as the range grows older, a classic sign of shares being absorbed”[Fraser 2017]

Deepvue’s screener lists relative strength, RVOL, ATR and dollar volume among its fields. It also has ready-made “Deepvue Leaders” (relative-strength leadership) and “Volatility Contraction” (tight bases, low ATR) presets you can stack your own filters on[deepvue.com/screener]. I don’t know its exact field names, so match the rows above to what you find.

The read, Steps 3–4, by eye. For each survivor: is there a range? Which phase is it in? Then run Wyckoff’s nine buying tests[ChartSchool], which by now read like a summary of this course:

  1. Downside objective reached
  2. PS, SC and ST (Phase A)
  3. Volume grows on rallies, shrinks on reactions
  4. The downtrend (supply) line broken
  5. Higher lows
  1. Higher highs
  2. Stronger than the market (the RS line)
  3. A base forming (a horizontal range)
  4. Upside potential ≥ 3× the risk to the stop

Test 1 uses a Point & Figure count, which this course hasn’t covered. Use the range-based targets from Lesson 8 instead. Test 9 is where a good stock can still be a bad trade.
