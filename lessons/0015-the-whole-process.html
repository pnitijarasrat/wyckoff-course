One chart, four steps: the Wyckoff 2.0 process

You have all the parts: phases and events, trading zones, Volume Profile levels, the value-area principles, and order flow. Part 7 of Wyckoff 2.0 puts them in a fixed order. First the context, then where to wait, then the scenario, then the orders. This lesson drills that order until you follow it without thinking.

Your win today

On any chart, write a complete plan in four lines: which of the four contexts price is in, which zone you’re waiting at, the scenario as “If X, then Y” with its alternative, and the three orders (entry, stop, target).

Why this matters to you

This is your whole mission in one routine: “build bullish and bearish scenarios… mark trading zones in advance… define a concrete entry, stop loss and take-profit.” The book calls Part 7 the summary of “the most solid principles” it has, and gives it as a four-step outline[2.0 pp.271, 276–277]. Lessons 16 and on are case studies on your own tickers, and every one of them will use these four steps.

The order matters. Context decides what you want to do (buy or sell). Zones decide where. The scenario decides what price must do first. Only then do you think about orders[pp.276–277, 290]. Order flow comes in last and smallest: it’s only for checking the trigger, “only when we are in a potential entry situation”[pp.275–276].

2 · Step 1: the four contexts

ContextWhat you seeWhat you doWyckoff zone (Lesson 8)
1 · Range, at the extremesA trend has stopped and price is going sideways. It’s at the top or bottom of the rangeLook for the turn at the edge: “buy low and sell high”. Same as the value-area range tradePhase C: the Spring or UTAD, or its test
2 · Range, insideA Phase C shakeout has probably happened. Price is inside a wide rangeTrade toward the far edge, only from the right side of the last HVN, and only with room for a good R:RPhase D inside the range (LPS / LPSY)
3 · Trend, at the value areaPrice has just broken out of the rangeFirst check it isn’t a shakeout that falls back in. If the break looks real, trade its testPhase D: the BUEC / LPSY test
4 · Trend, away from valuePrice is well outside the old range and trendingOnly trade with the trend. Wait for a pullback to a levelPhase E

Book: §7.1, pp.279–289. The one question to keep asking is “in what context is the market now?”[p.282]

The last HVN sets your side. Inside a wide range or in a trend, find the most recent High Volume Node. Above it, consider only longs: the HVN is the accumulation underneath you. Below it, only shorts. You consider the other side only when price breaks that HVN, or builds a structure the other way[pp.284, 289].

3 · Step 2: where you wait

The trigger is looked for “exclusively” at levels marked in advance[p.291]. The context tells you which:

ContextWait at
1 · ExtremesThe structure’s edge, where a shakeout would happen. The shakeout itself is the aggressive entry, its test the conservative one. Also the VAH/VAL, which often sit at the same place
2 · InsideAn LVN inside the range’s profile, or the range’s VPOC/VWAP once price has broken through it and comes back to test it
3 · At valueThe broken Creek or Ice (an LVN by nature), then the VAH/VAL. The range’s VPOC is the last level: a pullback deeper than that is back inside value, and the breakout idea goes “in quarantine”
4 · AwayThe VPOC of the last impulse, the weekly VWAP, LVNs. Ideally, a small structure forming on top of one of them

Book: §7.2, pp.290–295. More levels lined up = better. VPOC + VWAP together is the book’s favourite[p.295]. For you, as a daily trader, the book’s choice is a fixed-range profile on the structure, or a weekly/composite profile[pp.290–291]. Use completed profiles. A profile still forming keeps moving its levels.

4 · Step 3: the scenario

A scenario is the move price must make before you’ll look for a trigger. There are only two kinds[pp.296–298]:

One move

Price is already on the right side. You need one action that brings it to your zone. Example: a potential Spring has happened. Wait for one move down, the test, and look for the trigger there.

Two moves

You want to buy, but price is below your zone, or facing a key level. First it must get above the zone, then come back to test it. Example: price at the range top. Wait for the break, then the test.

Write the scenario as a continuous validation protocol: “If price breaks the Creek, then I wait for a test to buy. If instead the breakout fails, then I wait for a test the other way to sell”[p.299]. The second sentence is the alternative scenario. You always write one, so you can change your mind quickly when the market changes it for you.

Plan only one move at a time. This is why the book doesn’t use limit orders to enter. A buy limit sitting at the Spring’s test level bets on two moves: down to the test, then up to the Creek. But the test may never hold, and price may keep falling. Plan the move down. When price gets there, read it again, and only then plan the move up[pp.303–305].

5 · Step 4: management

The ruleOn your daily stock charts
TriggerA bar that shows intent in your direction: the SOS bar (wide, closes in its top third, relatively high volume) or SOW bar. If you have footprint data, check it for initiative[pp.300–301]Daily bars. The footprint is optional on stocks: about half of US volume trades off-exchange (Lesson 13)
Entry orderA stop order just beyond the trigger bar: a buy stop above its high, a sell stop below its low. It’s a final filter. If the bar was a trap and reverses at once, you often never get filled[p.302]Place it after the close for the next session. A gap through it fills at the open, which can be worse than your price: a stop becomes a market order when triggered[SEC]
Stop lossBeyond the trigger bar (#1) or beyond the whole turn (#2), and behind as many levels as possible: an LVN first, then a VWAP, the VAL/VAH, even the VPOC (#3) if the R:R still works[pp.305–308]Wider stop = fewer shares. The sizing is still 1% (Lesson 8)
TargetThe HVN in the trade’s direction marks the area. Its VPOC is the exact price. Recent nodes pull harder than old ones. Untested old VPOCs (naked VPOCs) are targets too. When nothing is to the left: earlier highs/lows and climaxes[pp.308–311]Build a composite profile over the last months to see the HVNs
When price goes without you

It will. You planned well, but there was no test, or no clean trigger. The book is blunt about it: don’t chase it. “If the price goes without us absolutely nothing happens.” Your read was right, which is the skill you’re building, and you’re not on the wrong side either. If the idea still holds, plan the next zone[pp.311–313].
