Case study: a real top, and when to get out

Lesson 16 was a real bottom: where to buy. This one is a real top, read from the seat you’re usually in: you already own the stock, you’re up, and the chart is starting to change. The question isn’t whether to buy. It’s when to get out, and when not to buy the dip.

Your win today

Read a real distribution checkpoint by checkpoint while you hold the stock, and sell into strength instead of at the breakdown. Then plan the Phase C short with levels you could have drawn that day.

Why this matters to you

Your mission names distribution as one of the six places a stock can be. Every real chart so far was an accumulation, and you mostly trade long. That makes a distribution the structure that costs you money: it’s where the gains from the markup go back. The book’s own walkthrough of a top says that once the sellers show their hand, “the urgency to exit is evident”[Classic p.238]. The skill is to see it before that urgency arrives.

1 · Three things that look different at a real top

The textbookA real daily stock chart
The climaxA wide up bar on climactic volumeOften an earnings gap up. The book’s BC is a rise “guided by favorable news”, with the well-informed selling into it[Classic pp.109–110]. On a stock, the news is usually the report
The upthrustsHighs near the BCThe range can slope up: each push makes a new high, so it looks like strength. Read the volume on each push, not the tilt (Lessons 3 and 9)
The breakdownA wide down bar through the IceOften a gap down after the next report. The book allows it: a SOW bar “could also be identified by a bearish gap”[Classic p.166]. A long held into it is filled at the open, not at your stop

The holder’s rule for a range that leans distribution (mine, built from Lessons 7–8): sell into strength at the top edge, especially into a potential UTAD. Keep a last-resort exit on a close under the Ice, because by then you’ve given back the whole height of the range.

How the case works. As in Lesson 16: no ticker and no dates until the end, and volume as a multiple of the stock’s 50-session median. The chart’s axis fits only the bars you can see, so empty space doesn’t give the ending away.
