Who couldn’t wait? Bid, ask and the column a trade prints in

You missed every question in Lesson 13’s “Which column?” drill. When every answer is wrong, the problem is usually one rule held backwards, not seven separate mistakes. This lesson rebuilds that rule from the order book up, then you call fourteen trades before they happen.

Your win today

For any trade, name its footprint column in two seconds by asking one question: who couldn’t wait? An impatient buyer means ASK. An impatient seller means BID. Nobody impatient means no trade yet.

Why this matters to you

Lesson 13’s whole pattern (absorption, then initiative) is read from which column the volume lands in. If the column is backwards in your head, every footprint, delta reading and “selling absorption” call flips too. The book says this is where most order-flow analysis goes wrong: “many analyses of order crossing are erroneous because they start from wrong premises”[2.0 p.171]. It also matters for your own trading. When your stop fills worse than its price, you are on the wrong side of this exact mechanism.

1 · The order book: two queues of people waiting

There isn’t one price for a stock. There are two[p.144]:

  • The BID is the queue of buyers who are waiting. They have placed buy limits below the market. The best bid is the highest of them, and it’s the best price you can sell at right now.
  • The ASK (also called the offer) is the queue of sellers who are waiting, with sell limits above the market. The best ask is the lowest of them, and it’s the best price you can buy at right now.

The gap between them is the spread. The book calls waiting orders passive, or “liquidity creators”[p.145]. Nothing trades while everyone waits.

2 · A trade needs someone who won’t wait

A trade happens only when someone sends an order that won’t wait. That’s a market order, or a stop, which becomes a market order the moment its price is touched[pp.152–153][SEC]. The book calls these aggressive orders, or “liquidity takers”[p.145].

  • An impatient buyer can’t wait for a seller to come down, so they take the best ask. The SEC’s own example is a market buy that fills at “the best offer price”[SEC]. The trade happens at the ask price.
  • An impatient seller takes the best bid. The trade happens at the bid price.

3 · The trap: “BID” means two different things

On the order book (waiting)On the footprint (traded)
BID columnBuyers waiting with limitsTrades that happened at the bid price, which only happens when a seller couldn’t wait
ASK columnSellers waiting with limitsTrades that happened at the ask price, which only happens when a buyer couldn’t wait

So the footprint column names the price the trade happened at, and that price tells you who was impatient: “the column on which those order crossovers will be displayed will depend on the order that initiated it”[p.169]. A buyer can land in the BID column. It happens every time a buyer waits with a limit and a seller comes and hits it.

Heads-up on the book. p.144 has a slip: it says sellers place “their pending sell (bid) orders” in the ASK column. Read “(bid)” as “(ask)”. Sellers wait on the ask. Don’t let that sentence undo this lesson.

The rule, in three steps

1 · Find the impatient ordera market order, or a stop that just triggered. Ignore the limit that was waiting
→
2 · Which way?impatient buyer → paid the ask → ASK
impatient seller → took the bid → BID
→
3 · Ignore the storyopening or closing, profit or loss, fund or retail: none of it changes the column

If no order is impatient (someone just placed a limit or a stop), nothing prints. The order joins a queue, or waits for its trigger.

Drill 1 · Call it, then watch it happen

One live order book. Eight orders arrive one after another, and each one changes the book for the next. Before each order is sent, say where it will print. Then watch the yellow flash. One cell changes on the left, where someone was waiting, and one on the right, where the trade printed.

Drill 2 · Mixed calls

No simulator now. Run the three steps in your head. Some questions give you a story, and some give you only a quote and a price, which is how you’d meet it on a tape.

Real quotes · Do it on your broker (~10 min)

This works without Deepvue having a footprint. Any broker that shows a live quote and Time & Sales will do.

  1. Read a quote. Open a liquid stock you own. Write down bid × ask and their sizes. Which side would a market buy of 100 shares fill on, and at what price?
  2. Classify 10 prints. Watch Time & Sales for a minute. For each print, write at ask, at bid or between. Many brokers colour them: green at the ask, red at the bid. Prints between are often off-exchange fills. That’s the ~51% of US stock volume from Lesson 13, and it’s why a stock footprint has to guess the side.
  3. Classify your own last 3 fills. For each one: order type, buy or sell, and which column it printed in.
  4. Paste it back like this: 
    MSFT quote: 412.30 (300) × 412.32 (200) → market buy fills 412.32 = ASK
Prints: ask ask bid between ask bid bid ask between ask
My fills: Oct 14 buy limit 410.00 → BID (a seller hit me)
          Oct 16 sell stop 405.00 → BID, filled 404.91 (stop = market order)
          Oct 17 buy market → ASK
