Twenty stocks, twenty profiles: where to pin each one

Your scan hands you twenty stocks, and each one needs a profile. But a profile pinned in the wrong place gives you levels that wander every day, and a three-week range doesn’t say as much as a three-month one. This lesson covers where to pin it, which daily changes to ignore, and how much to trust each range’s levels.

Your win today

For each stock on your watchlist, pin one profile to the right bars: start at the climax, end today or just before the breakout. Know why its levels move and when that movement means something. Judge how far to trust a range’s levels by its age.

Why this matters to you

Your mission says “add Volume Profile (POC, value area) to that analysis to find levels more objectively.” A level that shifts every time you scroll isn’t objective. You noticed two things. Your VAH and VAL change from day to day, and your watchlist mixes ranges that are three months old with ranges that are only a few weeks old. Both come down to which bars the profile counts. Lesson 11 gave you the rule, “from the time the rotation starts until just before the imbalance”[2.0 p.217]. This lesson applies it to a live watchlist, where most ranges haven’t finished yet.

1 · Why your levels move: three ways to pin a profile

Deepvue’s own guide names three kinds of volume profile[Deepvue]. The book describes the same three[2.0 pp.196–201]:

KindWhich bars it countsDoes it change day to day?
Visible rangeWhatever is on screenYes, for the wrong reason. Every scroll and zoom changes it, and so does every new day: a bar comes in on the right and one drops off the left. “If the chart is moved, the profile will change”[p.200].
AnchoredFrom a bar you pick, up to todayYes, for the right reason. Each new day adds one more day to the auction. The book calls these the developing VPOC and levels[p.210].
Fixed range (Deepvue: measured)Between two bars you pickNo. “The fixed range is not updated”[p.198].

When a visible-range profile moves, the window has slid, and that tells you nothing about the market. When an anchored profile moves, the window has grown: that’s new volume at new prices, which is information. If your VAH and VAL move when you scroll, you’re on a visible range. If they move only when a new day prints, you’re anchored.

Two things to watch as you drag. In the first three weeks the anchored levels jump around, and in the third month they rarely move. Then switch to visible range and step through days 30 to 34. As the climax and the first test drop out of the window, the VAL climbs about $2, twice as far as the anchored one. Nothing happened in the market. Only the window changed.

2 · Where to pin it

The stock is…Pin itStartEnd
Inside its rangeAnchoredThe climax (SC or BC)Today
Broken out, waiting for the testFixed rangeThe climaxThe bar before the breakout
Broken out, then back insideAnchored againThe climaxToday: the break was part of the range
  • Start at the stopping action, the SC or BC that opens Phase A (Lesson 4). Not in the trend before it. Trend bars carry heavy volume at prices the range never went back to, and they turn the profile into a P or b shape[pp.209–211].
  • End today while price is inside. The range is still building, so let the profile build with it.
  • Freeze the end at the breakout. The test you’re waiting for comes back to a level of the range, the VAH or VAL first, then the VPOC. A profile that keeps running drags those levels toward the breakout[pp.216–217].
  • One profile over overlapping value. If a newer range trades inside an older one’s value, pull one profile over both. Its levels “will have by their very nature a greater relevance”[p.198].

3 · Three weeks or three months

The book’s view is that the same behaviour plays out at every scale. A small structure and a big one differ in “the time consumption needed by the market to complete such a takeover process”[2.0 p.268]. Time matters for two reasons. The first is Wyckoff’s law of cause and effect: “the longer the period of time the market spends in a rank condition developing a campaign, the greater the distance the subsequent trend movement will travel”[Classic p.55]. A three-week range builds a smaller cause. The second is that a young profile hasn’t settled yet. On day n of a range, a new day adds roughly 1/n of the profile’s volume. I measured how far the VAH and VAL moved per day on three real ranges from earlier lessons:

Range ageANET (BC → UTAD, 61 bars)GOOGL (BC → Feb, 48 bars)META (SC → report, 16 bars)
Days 5–152.9% (worst day 6%)3.4% (worst 16%)4.0% (worst 9%)
Days 16–301.8%1.2%—
Days 46+0.6%1.3%—

The average daily move of the VAH and VAL, as a share of the range’s height on that day. Computed from daily bars, with each bar’s volume spread over its high–low (this course’s profile). A platform using intraday data will differ in detail.

This is three ranges and my own count, not the book’s. The working rule it supports is mine. Under about four weeks (20 bars), treat a profile’s levels as a zone, and lean on the Wyckoff events (SC, AR, ST) for stops and edges. From about two months on, the levels have settled, so a move in them means something: value shifting up or down inside the range.

So, for the two cases on your watchlist:

The few-week range is…Profile to plan withThe short one’s job
Inside a bigger range (a tight drift in its lower half, say)The big range, from its climaxPhase B resting, or a small structure to time an entry
A new step above an old range (after a markup)The new range, from the markup’s topIt’s the live structure. Keep the old range’s fixed profile under it as the next floor. The market looks for the most recent balance first[pp.182–183]

Real charts · Do it in Deepvue (~25 min)

  1. Check which kind you have. Open one stock with a profile on it. Scroll left, then back. Did the levels move? If yes, it’s a visible range. Look in the profile’s settings for an anchored or measured option.
  2. Sort the 20. Label each one inside, broke out or no range (trending). For no range, there’s no structure to profile, so keep the last range’s fixed profile as context.
  3. Pin five of them. Pick five that are inside or broke out. Anchor at the climax, and end today or on the bar before the breakout. Count the bars.
  4. Write the note in each one’s Deepvue watchlist note: 
    XYZ · range since SC Jul 14 (62 bars, mature) · anchored · VAH 48.20 · VPOC 46.10 · VAL 44.90
ABC · new step since Sep 22 (14 bars, young: zone only) · anchored · VA ≈ 112–118 · old range VAH 104.50 below
  5. Each day: check only the mature anchored ones. Did the VPOC move toward the top or the bottom of the range? Re-pin only when the structure changes: a breakout, a failed breakout, or a new range.
