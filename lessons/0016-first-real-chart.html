Case study: your first real chart

Every chart so far was built to teach one idea. This one wasn’t built at all. It’s a real US stock, with its real daily volume, played forward one checkpoint at a time. You run the four steps from Lesson 15 on it, and you hit the two things a textbook chart never shows you: gaps and earnings.

Your win today

Take a real accumulation from the selling climax to the breakout’s test, using only what you could see on each day, and finish with a sized order. Along the way, adjust the plan for an overnight gap and an earnings report.

Why this matters to you

Your mission says “given any US stock chart in Deepvue.” Synthetic charts were the practice. Real ones are the test. The book’s own case studies make the same point: “the market tends to present the same schemes but in different ways”, so you have to give structures “flexibility” and read what price does, not tick labels off[2.0 pp.314–315]. Its cases are intraday futures and forex. This one is a daily stock chart: your timeframe and your market.

1 · Three things that change on a real chart

The textbookA real daily stock chart
LabelsThe ST holds above the SC. The trigger is a clean SOS barThe ST can end a little below the SC. The book allows it: what matters most is less volume and narrower bars[Classic pp.123–124]. Triggers are often imperfect. Weigh the evidence (Lesson 7) instead of waiting for every box to be ticked[2.0 p.317]
GapsA buy stop fills at its priceA stock can open far above your stop price. The stop becomes a market order and fills at the open: the fill “can deviate significantly from the stop price in a fast-moving market”[SEC]. Your risk per share grows, and so does your loss if you’re wrong
EarningsNot covered. Futures and forex don’t report earningsA report can gap the stock past your stop overnight. A stop loss only limits the loss during the session. This rule is mine, not the book’s: before a report, hold only as many shares as you could afford to lose on a gap

How the case works. No ticker and no dates until the end, so hindsight can’t answer for you. Volume is given as a multiple of the stock’s median over the previous 50 sessions: 2.2× means more than double a normal day. Please don’t look it up halfway through.

Drill 1 · Replay: from the climax to the breakout

Five checkpoints. At each one, go through the four steps (context → zone → scenario → orders) and answer for now.

Drill 2 · Plan the breakout’s test

The last step: the BUEC. This time you place the stop and the target yourself, on the real chart, and size the trade for a $25,000 account at 1% risk.

Drill 3 · Recall: today and earlier

The last three cards come from Lessons 14, 3 and 15.

Real charts · Do it in Deepvue (~20 min)

That selling climax hit almost every US stock in the same week, so you can find it on your own watchlist.

  1. Open one stock you own or watch on the daily chart, March to June 2025.
  2. Find its climax and write five lines: 
    XYZ (daily)
SC:   Apr 7 · low $___ · volume ___
AR:   Apr _ · high $___ (this is the Creek)
ST:   Apr _ · low $___ · volume ___ → ST÷SC = __%
Break of the AR high?  yes / no · date
BUEC (quiet test of it)?  yes / no · low $___
  3. Paste me the five lines. I’ll check them against the stock’s daily data. If yours never broke its AR high, even better: tell me what it did instead.

Primary source: read this next (~15 min)

Villahermosa, Wyckoff 2.0, Part 8 “Case studies” (your Wyckoff 2.0.pdf, PDF pp.314–318, the intro and case 8.1). It’s a futures chart on 15-minute bars, but the read is the same as today’s: SC, AR, ST, then a Spring and its test, with volume falling through the range. Watch for where the author says the theory and the chart don’t match (p.317), and what he looks at instead. Then skim case 8.5 (pp.328–331): “prioritize the appearance of the initiative” over a perfect pattern.

The Secondary Test: Villahermosa, The Wyckoff Methodology in Depth (your Classic Wyckoff.pdf), ch.17, PDF pp.121–124. Gap fills and stop-limit orders: SEC Investor Bulletin, “Trading Basics”. Chart data: daily bars from Nasdaq.com historical quotes.
