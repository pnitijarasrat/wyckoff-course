Phase A: how a trend stops

Lesson 3 showed you a climax bar. Now you’ll see the four events that turn that bar into a trading range.

Your win today

On a chart where a trend has just stalled, label PS, SC, AR and ST (or PSY, BC, AR and ST at a top), draw the range they create, and say whether the stop looks strong or weak.

Why this matters to you

Phase A has one job: “to stop the previous trend movement and return the market to a state of balance … We move from a trend context to a range context.”[Classic p.180] For a stock trader, it answers two practical questions:

  • At a top (PSY, BC): when should I take profits on longs? The book calls the climax “the last clear opportunity to take profit”[p.105].
  • At a bottom (PS, SC): when should I stop shorting, and when not to buy yet? Buying the climax itself is “not recommended … as the assumed risk would be too high”[p.105].

The four events

#Bottom (accumulation)Top (distribution)What to look for
1PS Preliminary SupportPSY Preliminary SupplyThe first failed stop attempt. Volume jumps and price bounces, but the trend makes a new extreme afterwards. Treat it as the first entry of big traders[pp.93–94].
2SC Selling ClimaxBC Buying ClimaxThe stop that works, often the heaviest volume of the move. It can be one wide bar, a long-wicked bar, or “a set of bars with a relatively narrower range and with a high and constant volume”[p.103]. Sets one limit of the range[p.107].
3AR Automatic RallyAR Automatic ReactionThe biggest counter-move since the trend began. It starts on heavy volume, then ranges narrow and volume dries up[pp.112, 114]. Its extreme sets the other limit[p.115].
4ST Secondary TestST Secondary TestA return to the climax area “with a narrowing in the price ranges and a smaller volume than that seen in the Selling Climax.” Volume matters most, then range[pp.123–124]. Phase A ends here, and Phase B begins.
Every label is “potential” until the next event confirms it

A heavy-volume low is only a potential SC. You label it SC once you see the AR and ST: “when we look at the genuine Automatic Rally/Reaction and Secondary Test, we will automatically label the previous movement as Climax.”[p.103] If a climax is followed by sideways drift instead of a strong reaction, “the market will most likely continue in the direction of the previous trend.”[p.102]

Don’t force it

  • PS can be missing. The climax can do its job alone[p.101].
  • The climax may not be climactic. In a Selling Exhaustion, sellers just fade away. You see several ever-lower PS bounces on shrinking volume, and the final low comes on ordinary volume. You still label that low SC[pp.107–108].
  • What matters is the stop itself, not perfect labels. “The important thing is not that we know how to identify those 4 stop events, but that the market has objectively produced the stop of the trend movement.”[2.0 pp.13–14]

Reading the stop: early clues to what comes next

You won’t know whether the range is accumulation or redistribution until price leaves it (Lesson 1). But the AR and ST already give you clues. Add them up, then decide which way to lean[pp.123–124]:

ClueBuyers gaining (bullish)Sellers gaining (bearish)
AR sizeMuch bigger than earlier rallies in the decline, which “suggests a stronger bottom”[p.113]Short, overlapping bars on no volume: “we have to doubt … accumulation … think of a process of redistribution”[p.113]
Where the ST ends (bottom)In the upper half of the range[p.123][2.0 pp.73–74]Slightly below the SC low
ST volumeMuch lower than the SC: selling pressure has dried up[p.102]Still heavy: the test is “not valid”, so wait for another test or for the trend to resume[p.129]

At a top, flip the table. A short, overlapping AR on no volume, followed by an ST that pushes above the BC, points to re-accumulation, not distribution[p.114]. An ST that stalls low in the range points to sellers.

The Phase A read

  1. Trend first: is there a long trend to stop? No trend, no Phase A.
  2. Climax: find the heavy-volume extreme. Before it, look for earlier failed stop attempts (PS/PSY).
  3. Reaction: did a big counter-move follow? Its end is the AR. Draw horizontal lines at the climax extreme and the AR extreme. That’s your range.
  4. Test: did price return to the climax area on lighter volume and narrower bars? That’s the ST, and Phase A is done.
  5. Lean: grade the AR’s size and where and how the ST ended. Then decide what you’ll do: take profits, stop trading the old trend, or wait.

Drill 1 · Label Phase A

Click the bars in the order asked. Clicks snap to the nearest swing low or high. If you miss, you get a hint and can try again. When the chart is done, check what happened next.

Drill 2 · Strong or weak stop?

Each chart ends just after the ST, or where the ST should have been. Read the AR and the ST, then judge. All six questions have the same four options.

Drill 3 · Recall: today and earlier

Scroll so the tables are off-screen, then answer. The last two cards are from Lessons 3 and 2.

Real charts · Do it in Deepvue (~25 min)

  1. Find two daily charts where a decline has stopped and the stock has gone sideways for at least 3–4 weeks, plus one where an advance has stopped. Your watchlist from Lessons 1–3 is a good place to start: any stock you called “range after a fall” or “range after a rise”.
  2. On each one, mark PS/PSY, SC/BC, AR and ST with Deepvue’s text or arrow tool. Skip any event you can’t find honestly. Missing PS is normal.
  3. Draw two horizontal lines: one at the climax extreme and one at the AR extreme.
  4. Compare volume: is the ST’s volume clearly lower than the SC’s or BC’s? Did the AR travel further than the earlier counter-moves in the trend?
  5. Paste it back to me like this. I’ll check your dates against the price and volume data: 
    XYZ — downtrend from Mar; PS Jun 3 (2× vol), SC Jun 18 low $41.2 (4× vol),
      AR Jul 2 high $48.9, ST Jul 15 low $42.5 (0.8× vol)
    — AR = biggest rally since March; ST in lower half, on light volume
    — LEAN: stop looks valid, slightly bullish because …; my action: …
Primary source: read this next (~25 min)

Villahermosa, The Wyckoff Methodology in Depth (your Classic Wyckoff.pdf). Read Chapters 14–17 (PDF pp.93–128), one chapter per event, each covering how the event looks on the chart, the psychology behind it, and how to use it. Then read the one-page summary in Ch. 21 “Phase A” (p.180). The schematics on pp.38 and 42 (basic accumulation and distribution) show where these events sit in the full structure.

Second book: Villahermosa, Wyckoff 2.0, PDF pp.13–14 (don’t force the four stop events) and pp.73–74 (reading where the Phase A test ends). Cross-check: Bogomazov & Lipsett, “The Wyckoff Method: A Tutorial” (StockCharts ChartSchool). Its accumulation and distribution schematics label the same PS/SC/AR/ST and PSY/BC/AR/ST sequence, with short definitions of each.

Stuck? Ask me

Real charts are messier than these drills. There may be two candidate SCs, an AR that’s really two rallies, or a V-bottom with no range. Paste the ticker and dates and we’ll label it together. When in doubt, the book’s advice is to keep labels “potential” until the next event confirms them.
