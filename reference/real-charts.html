Real stock charts: labels, gaps, earnings

The book’s rules came from futures and forex. On daily US stock charts, three things need adjusting: how strictly you read labels, how you enter across gaps, and what you hold into earnings.

1 · Labels: flexible, volume first

  • The ST can undercut the SC. It leans slightly toward sellers, but what matters most, “in order of importance”, is less volume than the SC and narrower bars (Classic pp.123–124).
  • ST or Spring? The label matters less than the read: a new low with little effort means sellers are absent (2.0 p.317).
  • Triggers are often imperfect. If context, level and drying volume agree, an imperfect bar can do. Otherwise, wait for initiative (2.0 p.330).
  • Look left for confluence: an old profile’s VPOC on the Creek makes it a stronger level (2.0 p.295).

2 · Gaps and entry orders

OrderOn a gap through it
StopBecomes a market order and fills at the open. The fill “can deviate significantly from the stop price” (SEC)
Stop-limitBecomes a limit order at a price you choose (e.g. the stop + a little). A big gap doesn’t fill: you miss the trade, and that costs nothing (SEC)

If you were filled on a gap, redo the arithmetic: risk per share = fill − stop. With a fixed share count, a gap can turn 1% risk into 1.6% (Lesson 16: buy stop $507.00, filled at $528.52).

3 · Earnings (my rule, not the book’s)

  • A stop loss limits the loss only while the market is open. Overnight, the stock can open past it, and you’re filled at the open.
  • Before a report, ask “what do I lose on a large gap?”, not “where’s my stop?” Hold only the shares you could afford to lose on that gap, or none.
  • Check the report date before every new entry. A setup the week before earnings has two risks: the chart and the report.
