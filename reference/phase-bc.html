Phases B and C

Phase B rotates inside the box and tests both ends. Phase C is one false break beyond every earlier extreme, plus its test. A Spring or UTAD is only “potential” until the range breaks the other way.

Phase B tests (same action, label by lean)

ActionAccum.Distrib.
Pokes above the top, re-entersUAUT
Slips below the bottom, re-entersST as SOWmSOW

Unsure of the lean? Call it “ST in B”. A new extreme becomes the new edge of the box. Phase B should usually last longer than A and C; a short B means urgency (Classic pp.126–128, 182–183).

Bias for Phase C

  • UA earlier → a break of the lows is more likely a Spring.
  • mSOW earlier → a break of the highs is more likely a UTAD.
  • Structural failure (a test at one end, then price can’t reach the other end) → expect a breakout from the middle (LPS/LPSY), with no shakeout (2.0 pp.76–77).

Spring types

TypeBreakAction
#1 TSODeep, wide bars, heavy volumeMust be tested
#2Moderate, with volume and range risingWait for the test(s)
#3Slight, narrow bars, light volumeCan buy directly

Good test: narrower bars, less volume, holds above the Spring low. UTAD: moderate to heavy volume. Its test stalls below the UTAD high on less volume, and sometimes there’s no test (Classic pp.144–152).

Shakeout or real break?

  • Quick re-entry into the range → shakeout. Stays outside, and the retest fails at the old level → real break (p.141).
  • The shakeout must reach at least the opposite end of the range. If it can’t even show minor strength and then makes a new extreme, it’s just a test (2.0 pp.67, 80–81).
  • Best risk/reward in the whole structure: the stop goes just beyond the shakeout (Classic p.129).

Sources: Villahermosa, The Wyckoff Methodology in Depth, pp.125–153 (Phase B tests, Ch. 18 “Shaking”), pp.182–185 (Phases B and C), pp.39–41 (schematics). Wyckoff 2.0 pp.67, 75–81.
