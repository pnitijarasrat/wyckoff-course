Phase A

Four events turn a trend into a range. The climax sets one limit of the range and the reaction sets the other. Every label stays “potential” until the next event confirms it.

The four events

Bottom / TopLooks like
PS / PSYFirst stop attempt. Volume jumps, then the trend makes a new extreme anyway. Optional
SC / BCThe stop that works, usually on the heaviest volume. It can be one wide bar, a long wick, or a cluster of heavy bars. Sets range limit #1
ARThe biggest counter-move of the trend. It starts heavy, then volume and ranges dry up. Sets range limit #2
STA return to the climax area on less volume and narrower bars. Ends Phase A

Classic pp.93–128, 180. Selling or Buying Exhaustion is a climax without climactic volume: ever-lower PS bounces on shrinking volume, then a quiet final low (pp.107–111).

Early lean (add up the clues)

ClueBullishBearish
ARMuch bigger than earlier ralliesShort, overlapping, no volume
ST locationUpper half of the rangeBelow the SC low
ST volumeFar below the SCStill heavy: test invalid, so wait

Classic pp.113–114, 123–124, 129; 2.0 pp.73–74. At a top, an overlapping AR plus an ST above the BC suggests re-accumulation.

What to do

  • PS/PSY, SC/BC: stop trading in the old trend’s direction and take profits. Don’t open a position at the climax, because the risk is too high (p.97, 105).
  • No strong reaction after a climax? The trend probably continues (p.102).
  • After the ST: Phase B. Price rotates between the two limits.
  • Can’t find all four events? That’s fine. What matters is that the trend objectively stopped (2.0 pp.13–14).

Sources: Villahermosa, The Wyckoff Methodology in Depth, Chs. 14–17 and 21 (PDF pp.93–128, 180). Wyckoff 2.0 pp.13–14, 73–74. Bogomazov & Lipsett, “The Wyckoff Method: A Tutorial” (StockCharts ChartSchool).
