Sloping structures, SOT and structural failure

The labels serve the read. Don’t discard a stock because Phase A isn’t textbook. Ask who is gaining control, from the traces (2.0 pp.13–16).

Structural failure

Find the structure price respects (two edges, as many touches as possible). Expect it to keep going edge to edge. A failure is turning before the opposite edge.

What you see
▲ StrengthA decline that can’t reach the bottom
▼ WeaknessA rally that can’t reach the top

An LPS / LPSY is a structural failure. It’s one more trace, not a reversal signal. Edge to edge is just the structure working, so don’t score it. 2.0 pp.31–34.

Shortening of the Thrust (SOT)

Each new extreme travels a shorter distance past the previous one. Measure new low − previous low (or high − high).

CheckRule
Minimum3 thrusts; look from the 3rd or 4th
Heavy volume on the short thrustEffort without result: the other side is absorbing
Light volumeExhaustion: the trend side is withdrawing
More than 4 pushes, still shorteningMaybe too strong to fade
ConfirmationA strong move the other way on high volume, then join on the pullback
ContextThe last shakeout sets the bias: no SOT long after an Upthrust, no SOT short after a Spring

2.0 pp.35–37. Same idea as projection (Lesson 2), measured over several pushes.

Drawing a sloping range

  1. Start with horizontal edges from Phase A.
  2. If Phase B keeps leaving them, connect the highs or lows of Phases A–C, and copy the line to the other side.
  3. It should jump out and contain almost all the price action. A zone is fine.
  4. Keep the horizontal levels too.

Same events and phases as a flat range: tilt it level in your head. Slope up = buyers start with control; down = sellers. 2.0 pp.39–41.

The four kinds

StructureTell-tale traces
Accumulation ↗ strongestNo SOT on the highs. A UT-looking probe, then a pullback that can’t reach the bottom. Hard to buy: it always looks high. pp.42–45
Accumulation ↘SOT on the lows (falling volume). A pullback holds in the middle. Confirm with a strong break of the channel, then buy the pullback. pp.45–49
Distribution ↘ weakestNo SOT, no structural failure. Heavy drops, then a UTAD (top overshoot or local high). Fast. Hard to short: it always looks low. pp.49–52
Distribution ↗SOT on the highs (UTAD often heavy). A rally can’t reach the top. pp.53–56

The slope alone never decides it; the traces do. Failed structures (2.0 pp.24–30): every trace points one way, and the test after the breakout fails. Trade in the direction of the last shakeout.
