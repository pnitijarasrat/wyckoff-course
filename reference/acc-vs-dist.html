Accumulation or distribution? The scorecard

After a rise, a range is either a potential re-accumulation or a potential distribution. After a fall, it’s either accumulation or redistribution. Phase A looks the same in both. Score each trace as it appears, give the latest one the most weight, and call it a lean, never a certainty.

Which pair are you choosing between?

Comes afterBullish outcomeBearish outcome
An uptrendRe-accumulation → more markupDistribution → markdown
A downtrendAccumulation → markupRedistribution → more markdown

Re-accumulation is the same process as accumulation. It just starts by stopping a rise (Classic p.77). It’s needed because stock drifts into weak hands during a trend. The more stock weak hands hold, the longer the range lasts (p.78).

The four footprints, in order

Trace▲ Strength▼ Weakness
1 · ST (Phase A)Ends in the upper half, or above the topEnds in the lower half, or below the bottom
2 · Phase B testsPrice tests the topPrice tests the bottom
3 · Phase C shakeout (dominant)A Spring that re-enters the rangeA UTAD that re-enters the range
4 · Phase DWide up bars, rising volume, reaching the topWide down bars, rising volume, reaching the bottom

2.0 pp.73–82. Draw a midline first. A trace that ends right on the midline is no clear sign.

The range’s character

Accum. / re-accum.Distrib. / redistrib.
Volume and volatilityShrinkStay high and constant
Low-volume tests ofThe topThe bottom
Wider, smoother movesUpDown
Late in the rangeRising highs and lowsFalling highs and lows

Classic pp.76, 84. Redistribution stays volatile right to the end (p.87).

Rules for weighing

  • The latest trace weighs most. A clear shakeout outweighs the earlier traces (2.0 p.78).
  • Read what follows a Phase B test. A test at one end followed by a structural failure (price can’t reach the other end) points to a breakout from the middle, with no shakeout (2.0 pp.76–77).
  • The AR counts. An AR that is short, overlapping and light on volume shows the other side has no intent. After a fall, think redistribution (Classic pp.113–114).
  • No shakeout isn’t neutral. A quiet lower high in Phase C is an LPSY, which is a weakness trace (2.0 p.77).
  • Always name what would make you wrong.
