Analyse a chart from zero: top down

Clean chart. Start on the weekly. Drop down only when price is at an edge, to find the minor structure the major one is waiting for. Always keep both scenarios ready (2.0 pp.89–103).

Step 1 · Weekly verdict (about 2 minutes)

The weekly showsVerdict
A trend still running, no stopping actionWait for a trend stop (Phase A)
Phase B, price mid-rangeWait for a range extreme
Cause built, price at an edge (potential breakout or shakeout)Drop to the daily, the ideal context
Nothing clearSkip it. Try a higher timeframe, then another stock

Before you start: delete old labels and keep the levels. Monthly only if the weekly isn’t enough. Note the trading zones above and below as targets (Volume Profile from Lesson 11; until then, range edges and old swing highs and lows). Market first: check the index trend too (Wyckoff’s step 1). 2.0 pp.89–93.

Step 2 · The structure’s roadmap

  • Phase B → wait for price at the extremes
  • Shakeout confirmed → expect the opposite extreme, with momentum
  • Genuine breakout → wait for the test of the broken structure
  • Plan only the next move, never beyond (2.0 pp.68, 91)

Step 3 · Drop down: what to look for

Weekly positionDaily: look forFlips to
Potential Spring (C)Minor accumulation = the SpringMinor distribution = real breakdown → short
Potential BUEC (D)Minor re-accumulation = the testMinor distribution = it was an Upthrust → short
Markup (E)Fast minor accumulations to joinMinor distribution = a new weekly BC (see below)

Mirror every row for shorts. Larger structures beat smaller ones. The lower you go, the more noise. Stocks: weekly → daily, with the hourly only to time an entry. 2.0 pp.93–96.

Going up (bottom-up)

In Phase E, a daily distribution = the weekly BC, and its fall = the weekly AR. That means a weekly range is coming, not straight markdown. Daily accumulation in a fall = the weekly SC → AR. A minor structure that fails at its BUEC can become part of a larger one (2.0 pp.97–100).

When it isn’t clear

Go up a timeframe. Stay at the clearest one and don’t go lower. Still unclear? Change stock. One stock on a 1-minute chart is “the perfect combination for ruin” (2.0 pp.101–103).

A line is a location, not a signal. At a line, wait for a structure (2.0 pp.62–65).

Source: Villahermosa, Wyckoff 2.0, Part 2 (PDF pages). See also Lesson 10 · Phases B–C · Trade-plan cheat sheet · Glossary
