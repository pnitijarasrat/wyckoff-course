Effort vs result

Effort = volume. Result = price. When they agree (harmony), expect continuation. When they disagree (divergence), watch for a turn. Volume is always relative.

Five places to read it

ScaleHarmonyDivergence
BarWide range + high vol; narrow + lowNarrow range + high vol (absorption); wide + low
Bars afterBig-volume bar → continuationBig-volume bar → turn
SwingsImpulses ↑ vol, pullbacks ↓ volNew extreme on ↓ vol; pullback on ↑ vol
LevelsBreaks on volume and holdsBreaks on volume and fails (false break)
Trend endClimax: very heavy volume, little progress. Lack of interest: very light volume at lows (or highs)

Classic pp.64–70. Divergence size predicts turn size (p.62).

The read

  1. Find the stand-out volume: biggest bars and biggest waves.
  2. Check the result: how far did price go, and where did it close?
  3. Check what came next: continuation or turn? Did the break hold?
  4. Verdict: harmony → continuation or divergence, because… → watch for a turn. An alert, not a trade.

Shortening push + volume

Shorter push on…Means
heavy volumeBig effort, little reward. The other side is absorbing
light volumeExhaustion. The trend side is withdrawing

Wyckoff 2.0 p.36. Look for it from the 3rd–4th push on.

Ignore or discount: earnings days, index rebalances and opex. They inflate volume without telling you anything about intent.

Sources: Villahermosa, The Wyckoff Methodology in Depth, Ch. 9 (PDF pp.61–70). Wyckoff 2.0 pp.36, 84–86. Bogomazov & Lipsett, “The Wyckoff Method: A Tutorial” (StockCharts ChartSchool).
