Order flow, lightly

Order flow shows who was aggressive at each price inside a bar. It can’t show why. Look at it only at a zone you already marked, under the Wyckoff context (2.0 pp.175, 179, 247).

Who couldn’t wait? (L14)

Order book = waitingFootprint = traded
BIDbuy limits queued below price; best bid = best price to selltrades at the bid price = an impatient seller
ASKsell limits queued above price; best ask = best price to buytrades at the ask price = an impatient buyer

(1) Find the market order or triggered stop; ignore the waiting limit. (2) Impatient buyer → ASK, impatient seller → BID. (3) Ignore the story (opening/closing, profit/loss). No impatient order → nothing prints. A filled buy limit prints on the BID; a filled sell limit on the ASK (2.0 pp.144–145, 169–171).

How a trade prints

AggressorMeetsColumn
Market or triggered-stop buyresting sell limitASK
Market or triggered-stop sellresting buy limitBID
  • The order-flow problem: a short’s stop-loss prints on the ASK, and a long’s take-profit limit is passive. Both are exits, not new pressure. The column shows aggression, not intent (pp.169–173).
  • Passive orders stop moves. Only aggressive orders move price (p.255).
  • US stocks: ~51% of 2025 volume traded off-exchange (Cboe), so the bid/ask side is often inferred, and stock footprints are noisier than futures.

Reading a footprint

  • Diagonal, not across. Compare ASK[row] with BID[row − 1], and BID[row] with ASK[row + 1] (pp.249–250).
  • Imbalance: ≥ 3× its diagonal partner (the book allows 200–400%), plus a minimum size (p.251).
  • Delta = ASK − BID volume. Delta against the candle (+ on a failed up bar) = divergence = possible absorption, if it’s at a zone (pp.175–177).

The turn: exhaustion → absorption → initiative

AbsorptionInitiative
Volumehighhigh
Imbalancescolumn of the move being stopped, at its extremecolumn of the new direction, at the start of the bar’s move
Closeagainst themwith them, at the extreme (= SOS/SOW bar)
Confirmed byno continuationan immediate follow-through

2.0 pp.253–261. Buying absorption + selling initiative = bearish turn. Selling absorption + buying initiative = bullish turn.

Continuation: control → test of control

  • Control: initiative mid-move. Imbalances (ideally 2+) on a wide, heavy bar closing in its far third (pp.262–263).
  • Test of control: a quiet, low-volume return to that zone, then fresh initiative = entry (pp.264–265).
