Volume Profile on a Wyckoff structure

Vertical volume tells you when the big players traded. The profile tells you where. Price explores, time shows acceptance, and volume confirms it: price + time + volume = value (2.0 pp.133–135, 202–203).

The five levels

LevelMeaningUse
VPOCThe price with the most volume, the fairest priceA magnet, so don’t open trades at it. Price above = buyers in control, below = sellers
VAH / VALEdges of the value area (~70% of the volume; the book uses 68.2%)Support/resistance. The first level for the test after a breakout
HVNA peak: old balanceA magnet → targets
LVNA valley: rejectionV-turn or a fast run through → entries; stops go beyond it

Range edges are LVNs by nature. The market’s memory is short: the most recent balance area is the one price looks for first. Finished auction (the end of the profile tapers off) = rejection. Unfinished (it ends abruptly in a fat node) = price will likely come back. If the bottom is unfinished, put a long breakout on hold. When in doubt, treat it as finished. 2.0 pp.182–195, 215.

Which profile

  • Fixed range over the structure: from where the rotation starts (the stopping action) to just before the breakout (pp.196–198, 217)
  • Composite (1–2 years, or everything on screen): HVNs and LVNs above and below price, for targets (pp.200–201)
  • Weekly VWAP as a companion level. Price above both the VWAP and the VPOC = buyers in control, below both = sellers (pp.190–192, 221–222)

With Wyckoff

SituationProfile read
Breakout up (SOS/JAC)Wait for the BUEC at the VAH, then the VPOC
Breakdown (MSOW)Wait for the LPSY at the VAL, then the VPOC
Messy range, unclear edgesUse the VAH and VAL as the structure’s edges
A trend is runningFavour the direction of the last HVN of the move. Only take the other side once price breaks it
Potential SpringGets back above the VAL (and VWAP) = long scenario. Can’t = weakness, treat it as distribution
P profile (thin stem, fat top)Up move, then a range → re-accumulation or distribution
b profile (fat bottom, thin top)Down move, then a range → accumulation or redistribution

2.0 pp.209–223.

The plan

Entry at an operating level (VAH/VAL, VPOC, VWAP) with a trigger bar. Stop beyond an LVN (earlier rejection). Target at the next HVN (old balance). The more operating levels on your side, the better (2.0 pp.220, 235).

On daily stock charts the volume is spread across each bar’s high–low, so profiles are smoother than intraday ones. The levels land close. Lesson 11’s drills compute profiles exactly this way.
