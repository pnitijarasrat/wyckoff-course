Wyckoff glossary

The course’s single source of vocabulary. Lessons use these terms exactly as defined here.

Sources: Villahermosa, The Wyckoff Methodology in Depth (“Classic”) and Wyckoff 2.0 (“2.0”). Page numbers are PDF pages. ✓ = taught so far; others are previews.

Context & the price cycle

TermMeaningSource
UptrendRising highs and rising lows. Demand is more aggressive than supply.Classic p.17✓ L1
DowntrendFalling highs and falling lows. Supply is more aggressive than demand.Classic p.18✓ L1
Trading range (TR)Highs and lows fluctuate inside a band. Relative balance, where a cause is built.Classic p.33✓ L1
AccumulationA range after a fall in which large traders absorb stock from weak hands. Leads to markup.Classic p.38, 72✓ L1
MarkupThe uptrend that follows accumulation.Classic p.14; Fraser✓ L1
DistributionA range after a rise in which large traders sell stock to weak hands. Leads to markdown.Classic p.42, 80✓ L1
MarkdownThe downtrend that follows distribution.Classic p.14; Fraser✓ L1
Re‑accumulationA range inside an uptrend, followed by more markup. Starts like distribution, and is told apart from it by the four footprints.Classic p.77–79; 2.0 pp.72–82✓ L1, L7
RedistributionA range inside a downtrend, followed by more markdown. Starts like accumulation, and stays volatile right to the end.Classic p.86–87✓ L1, L7
Buying / selling / neutral positionAccumulation or uptrend = buying position. Distribution or downtrend = selling position. No campaign = neutral.Classic p.16✓ L1
Cause & effectA range (cause) produces a trend (effect) in proportion to it. A range is only identified by its effect.Classic p.54; 2.0 p.24✓ L1
Major / minor structureA range on the higher timeframe (major) and a smaller range on a lower timeframe that sits inside one of its events (minor). A confirmed minor accumulation at a weekly potential Spring is the Spring. When they disagree, the larger structure gets more weight.2.0 pp.93–96✓ L10
Top-down (lowering temporality)Context analysis: read the higher timeframe first (usually the weekly), then go down only when price is at an edge, looking for a minor structure that fits.2.0 pp.89–96✓ L10
Bottom-up (rising temporality)Spotting a structure first: a minor structure on a low timeframe can become a major event, e.g. a daily distribution in Phase E = the weekly BC, and its effect = the weekly AR.2.0 pp.97–98✓ L10
Wyckoff’s five stepsStock selection in order: (1) the market’s position and probable trend, (2) stocks in harmony with it, (3) a cause big enough for the target, (4) readiness to move (the nine buying/selling tests), (5) timing with a turn in the index.ChartSchool tutorial✓ L19
RS line (relative strength)The stock’s close ÷ the index’s close (usually the S&P 500). Rising = beating the market, even while the price falls. Rank it from the market’s turn, not over the fall.ChartSchool; Fraser 2018✓ L19
In gearFraser: price above a rising 39-week average and the RS line above its own, or price and RS making higher lows together. Trends persist more often when they agree.Fraser 2017, 2018✓ L19
Composite Man / Composite OperatorA heuristic: imagine all price action as the work of one large operator, and read their intent.ChartSchool tutorial
Strong hands / weak handsWell-informed professionals vs. less-informed traders (often retail).Classic p.38

Trends & lines

TermMeaningSource
ImpulseA wave in the trend’s direction.Classic p.17✓ L2
Pullback (reaction)A wave against the trend, between two impulses.Classic p.17✓ L2
SpeedThe angle of a move. Flatter than the previous one = weakness.Classic p.21✓ L2
ProjectionThe distance an impulse travels compared with the previous one. Shorter = weakness.Classic pp.22–23✓ L2
DepthThe distance a pullback travels compared with the previous one. Deeper = weakness.Classic pp.23–24✓ L2
Demand lineUptrend line through two rising swing lows, where buyers are expected.Classic p.26✓ L2
Supply lineDowntrend line through two falling swing highs, where sellers are expected.Classic p.26✓ L2
Overbought lineThe demand line copied parallel through the high between its two lows. Exceeding it = overbought.Classic p.28✓ L2
Oversold lineThe supply line copied parallel through the low between its two highs. Exceeding it = oversold.Classic pp.28–29✓ L2
ChannelA trend line plus its parallel. Ideally it has several touches and holds most of the price.Classic p.27✓ L2
StrideFraser’s word for the pace a trend line captures.Fraser, “Trendapalooza”✓ L2
Support / resistanceA horizontal line through ≥2 lows / highs. Once broken, they tend to swap roles.Classic p.25

The three laws

LawMeaningSource
Supply & demandPrice moves when one side takes the initiative (market orders). Passive limit orders can stop a move but can’t start one.Classic p.47–53
Cause & effectSee above. The size of the move is proportional to the time spent building the cause.Classic p.54–60✓ L1
Effort vs resultVolume (effort) should agree with price movement (result). Divergence warns of a turn.Classic p.61–70✓ L3

Volume: effort & result

TermMeaningSource
Effort / resultEffort = volume; result = price movement.Classic p.61✓ L3
HarmonyEffort and result agree (big volume, big move; small volume, small move). Suggests continuation.Classic p.62✓ L3
DivergenceEffort and result disagree. Suggests weakness and a possible turn, sized in proportion to the divergence.Classic p.62✓ L3
AbsorptionHeavy volume with little price progress: passive orders on the other side soak up the aggressive ones. On a footprint (“takeover”): imbalances in the column of the move being stopped, with the close against them.Classic p.65; ChartSchool; 2.0 pp.253–255✓ L3, L13
Climactic volumeVery heavy relative volume after a long trend, especially with small progress. Signals the trend is nearly over.Classic p.69✓ L3
Lack of interestVery light volume at lows after a fall (no selling pressure) or at highs after a rise (no buying pressure).Classic pp.69–70✓ L3
Weis waveTotal volume of each price swing, so you can compare swings against each other (David Weis).Classic p.67; 2.0 p.84✓ L3
Effective vs false breakA level breaks on volume and holds (harmony) or falls back (divergence: the volume worked in the opposite direction).Classic p.68✓ L3

Structure levels

TermMeaningSource
CreekThe resistance of an accumulation or re-accumulation range, set by the AR high and Phase B highs.Classic p.38✓ L6
IceThe support of a distribution or redistribution range, set by the AR low and Phase B lows.Classic p.42✓ L6
CHoCH (Change of Character)A shift of environment: trend → range (Phase A), then range → trend (from the Phase C test to the SOS/SOW).Classic p.38, 42

Event definitions from Classic pp.38–44; Phase A detail pp.93–128.

TermMeaningSource
Selling / Buying ExhaustionA climax without climactic volume: the trend side fades away (ever-lower PS or ever-higher PSY on shrinking volume). Still labelled SC / BC.Classic pp.107–111✓ L4
Potential (event)Any event label before the next event confirms it. An SC is only confirmed by the AR and ST that follow.Classic pp.99, 103✓ L4
ST in BA neutral label for any Phase B test at either end, when you can’t yet tell accumulation from distribution.Classic p.128✓ L5
Shakeout (Phase C test)A false break of a liquidity zone that traps breakout traders and then re-enters the range. Spring at the bottom, UTAD at the top.Classic pp.131–142✓ L5
Terminal Shakeout (TSO)Spring #1: a deep break on heavy volume with fast recovery. It must be tested.Classic p.145✓ L5
Potential Spring / UTADA shakeout before the range has broken the other way. If it can’t reach the opposite end, relabel it a test.Classic p.142; 2.0 p.67✓ L5
Structural failurePrice has been moving edge to edge of a structure (flat or sloping), then turns before reaching the opposite edge. A decline that can’t reach the bottom = strength. A rally that can’t reach the top = weakness. An LPS/LPSY is one: a breakout from the middle without a shakeout.2.0 pp.31–34, 76–77✓ L5, L9
Shortening of the Thrust (SOT)Each new extreme travels less past the previous one (at least three thrusts): the trend is losing momentum. Heavy volume on the short thrust = absorption by the other side; light = exhaustion. Confirmed by a strong move the other way on volume.2.0 pp.35–37✓ L9
Sloping structureA range whose edges tilt. Same events and phases as a flat range. Up = buyers start with control, down = sellers. Four kinds: accumulation up/down, distribution up/down. SOT and structural failure tell them apart.2.0 pp.38–56✓ L9
Failed structureEvery trace points one way, but the test after the breakout fails and the range resolves the other way. A failed accumulation is a distribution, and vice versa.2.0 pp.24–30L9 (reading)
Breakout vs confirmationThe breakout (SOS/MSOW) is only an alert, not a trade. The trade is the confirmation test (LPS/BUEC, LPSY/FTI): narrow, quiet, staying outside the range. A heavy test means wait for more tests, and a wide heavy re-entry means the break failed (UT/Spring).Classic pp.154–160, 167–171✓ L6
SOS bar / SOW barA wide bar closing at its extreme on rising volume (or a gap). Used as the entry trigger at a test.Classic pp.162, 166, 171✓ L6
Four footprintsThe traces used to lean accumulation or distribution, in order: the ST in Phase A (upper or lower half), the Phase B tests (top or bottom) and what follows them, the Phase C shakeout (dominant), and Phase D price and volume. The latest trace weighs most.2.0 pp.72–82✓ L7
Ordinary ShakeoutA sharp, wide drop inside an uptrend without much of a range before it, which shakes out a re-accumulation. Its volume can be high, medium or low.Classic p.148✓ L7
Trading zonesThe only three places the book trades: Phase C (the shakeout or its test), Phase D (inside the range, and the breakout test) and Phase E (the trend outside the range). The earlier the zone, the better the R:R and the lower the reliability.Classic pp.198–207✓ L8
Significant barThe entry trigger. Compared with recent bars it has a wider range and higher volume, closes in the half of the bar in the direction of the move, and closes beyond a recent resistance or support. The SOS bar and SOW bar are examples.Classic pp.208–209✓ L8
Reversal of movementControl has flipped when a bar of the opposite intent closes beyond the far end of the last significant bar.Classic pp.210–211✓ L8
Liquidity zoneA level where pending orders cluster (earlier highs and lows, and a structure’s SC, AR or BC). It pulls price toward it, which makes it the natural place for a target.Classic pp.196–197, 224✓ L8
R:R (risk:reward)The distance to the target divided by the distance to the stop. The book avoids trades below 1:1.Classic p.214✓ L8
Fixed-fractional sizingRisk the same percentage (≤ 1%) on every trade: shares = (account × 1%) ÷ (entry − stop).Classic p.214✓ L8
Valid / invalid testReturning to a high-volume area on low volume = valid (that side has dried up). On still-high volume = not valid; wait.Classic p.129✓ L4
Value (auction)Price + time + volume. Price explores, time spent shows acceptance, volume confirms it. Horizontal development = balance (a range). Vertical = imbalance (a trend, searching for new value).2.0 pp.133–137✓ L11
Acceptance / rejectionOutside the old value: price holds, consumes time and builds volume = acceptance. A quick, sharp return to the old value = rejection.2.0 p.137✓ L11
Volume ProfileVolume plotted by price (a sideways histogram) instead of by time. Shows where the big players traded, not when.2.0 pp.180–184, 202–203✓ L11
VPOCVolume Point of Control: the price with the most volume, the fairest price. Price above it = buyers in control, below = sellers.2.0 pp.189–190✓ L11
Value Area (VAH / VAL)The band around the VPOC holding ~70% of the volume (the book uses 68.2%). Its high and low act as support and resistance, and are the first place to look for the test after a breakout.2.0 pp.185–186, 216–217✓ L11
HVN / LVNHigh / Low Volume Node: a peak (old balance, a magnet → targets) or a valley (rejection → entries, and stops beyond it). Range edges are LVNs.2.0 pp.192–195, 215, 235✓ L11
Finished / unfinished auctionThe end of a profile tapers off thinly (finished: rejection), or stops abruptly in a fat node (unfinished: price will likely come back). When in doubt, treat it as finished.2.0 pp.186–188✓ L11
Fixed range / composite profileFixed range: over chosen bars (a structure, from the stopping action to just before the breakout). Composite: a long period, for the HVNs and LVNs around price.2.0 pp.196–201, 217✓ L11
Anchored / visible-range profileAnchored: start pinned (the SC or BC), end = today, so it changes daily as the range builds (the developing value area). Visible range: whatever bars are on screen, so it changes when you scroll, zoom, or a day passes. For a structure, anchor while inside; freeze the end (fixed range) at the breakout.2.0 pp.198, 200, 210, 217✓ L20
Range ageHow many bars the range's profile holds. Each new day is about 1/n of its volume, so a young range (under ~4 weeks) has provisional levels: lean on the Wyckoff edges. A mature one (2–3 months) has settled levels, and a move in them means value is shifting.Lesson 20 (my count, 3 real ranges)✓ L20
VWAPVolume-weighted average price for a period (session, week, month). Institutions judge their fills against it. Above both the VWAP and the VPOC = buyers in control.2.0 pp.190–192, 221–222✓ L11
P / b profileP: a thin stem and a fat top (rally, then range → re-accumulation or distribution). b: the mirror (fall, then range → accumulation or redistribution).2.0 pp.209–213✓ L11
Reference profileThe profile whose value area you judge price against. Intraday: the previous session. Swing: the fixed range over the current structure, or a weekly/monthly/composite profile.2.0 p.237✓ L12
Trading range principlePrice inside the value area → fade the edges (buy the VAL, sell the VAH). Minimum target the VPOC, ambitious target the opposite edge.2.0 pp.237–238✓ L12
Reversion principlePrice from outside gets back into the value area and is accepted → trade to the opposite edge, managing at the VPOC. Adapted from Market Profile’s “80% rule” (a name, not audited odds).2.0 pp.239–240, 245✓ L12
Continuation principlePrice tries to get into the value area and is rejected → trade away from value. The test trade after a breakout (BUEC/LPSY at the VAH/VAL).2.0 pp.242–243✓ L12
Failed reversionPrice got back in but was strongly rejected at the VPOC → the reversion is cancelled. Back out through the edge = continuation; a real break of the VPOC = reversion again.2.0 pp.244–245✓ L12
Aggressive / passive orderMarket and triggered stop orders are aggressive: they take the best price now. Limit orders are passive: they wait in the book. Every trade is one of each. Passive orders can stop a move; only aggression moves price.2.0 pp.152–153, 167–169, 255✓ L13
Bid / ask (quote)Bid = the queue of buyers waiting with limit orders; the best (highest) bid is the best price you can sell at now. Ask (offer) = sellers waiting; the best (lowest) ask is the best price you can buy at now. The gap is the spread. Quoted as bid × ask.2.0 p.144; SEC Trading Basics✓ L14
BID / ASK columnWhere an executed trade prints: ASK when the aggressor was a buyer, BID when the aggressor was a seller. It shows who was in a hurry, not why (stop-losses and take-profits print there too). That's “the order-flow problem”. Not the same as the order book's bid/ask: the book shows who is waiting, the footprint who couldn't wait.2.0 pp.169–173✓ L13, L14
FootprintA candle split into price rows showing BID × ASK volume at each price. Read it diagonally: ASK[row] vs BID[row − 1].2.0 pp.163–164, 249–250✓ L13
DeltaASK volume − BID volume for a bar. Delta against the candle's direction (a divergence) hints at absorption, but only at a zone.2.0 pp.164–165, 175–177✓ L13
ImbalanceA footprint cell at least 2–4× (course: 3×) its diagonal partner, above a minimum size.2.0 pp.251–252✓ L13
InitiativeBig aggressive orders that move price: imbalances in the new direction's column, with the close, wide range, high volume. The footprint view of the SOS/SOW bar, and the entry trigger.2.0 pp.255–257✓ L13
Control / test of controlControl: initiative mid-move (imbalances on a wide, heavy bar closing in its far third). On daily charts, the VPOC of the impulse leg or an HVN. Test: a quiet return to it, then fresh initiative = entry.2.0 pp.262–265, 269–270✓ L13
FractalityThe same turn (absorption → initiative) at every scale: a few footprint candles, a P/b session profile, or a daily accumulation/distribution range and its breakout.2.0 pp.268–270✓ L13
Operating contextWhere price is, which decides the strategy: (1) range, at the extremes; (2) range, inside; (3) trend, interacting with the value area (the breakout's test); (4) trend, away from value (pullbacks only).2.0 pp.279–289✓ L15
Last HVN ruleThe most recent High Volume Node sets the side: above it, longs only; below it, shorts only, until it breaks or a structure forms the other way.2.0 pp.284, 289✓ L15
One-move / two-move scenarioOne move: price is on the right side and only has to come to your zone. Two moves: it must first get past the zone, then test it. Plan only one move at a time.2.0 pp.296–298, 303–304✓ L15
Continuous validation protocolWriting the scenario as “If X, then Y”, always with an alternative scenario the other way, so you can change bias quickly.2.0 p.299✓ L15
Stop-order entryEntering with a buy stop above the trigger bar's high (sell stop below its low), as a final filter that the move is continuing. Not a limit order at the expected turn.2.0 pp.302–304✓ L15
Naked VPOCA previous profile's final VPOC that price hasn't returned to yet. Used as a target.2.0 pp.310–311✓ L15
