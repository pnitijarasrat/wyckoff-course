The Wyckoff 2.0 process

Four steps, always in this order: context → zones → scenario → management. Context decides buy or sell, zones decide where, the scenario decides what price must do first, and the orders come last (2.0 pp.276–277).

1 · Context → 2 · Where to wait

ContextDoWait at
1 Range, extremesFade the edge (Phase C)Shakeout (aggressive) or its test (conservative); VAH/VAL
2 Range, insideToward the far edge (Phase D), only from the right side of the last HVN and with roomLVN inside the range; the range VPOC/VWAP after a break and test
3 Trend, at valueCheck it isn’t a shakeout, then trade the breakout’s testBroken Creek/Ice (an LVN), then VAH/VAL. Range VPOC = last level, deeper = “quarantine”
4 Trend, awayWith the trend only, on a pullback (Phase E)VPOC of the last impulse, weekly VWAP, LVN; ideally a small structure on one

pp.279–295. Last HVN rule: above the most recent HVN → longs only; below → shorts only. Change sides only when it breaks or a structure forms the other way (pp.284, 289). More levels lined up = better; VPOC + VWAP is the best pair (p.295).

3 · Scenario

  • One move: price is on the right side and just has to come to your zone (e.g. a potential Spring → wait for its test).
  • Two moves: price is on the wrong side of the zone or facing a key level → first past it, then the test (e.g. at the Creek → break, then test).
  • Write it as If X, then Y, plus the alternative: “If it breaks the Creek, I buy the test. If the break fails, I sell the test the other way” (p.299).
  • Plan one move at a time. A limit order at the expected turn bets on two (pp.303–304).

4 · Management

Rule
TriggerSOS bar / SOW bar at the zone: wide, closes in its far third, relatively high volume. Footprint (optional): initiative (pp.300–301)
EntryStop order just beyond the trigger bar: buy stop above its high, sell stop below its low. A trap that reverses often never fills you (p.302)
Stop#1 beyond the trigger bar · #2 beyond the turn · #3 beyond the VPOC if the R:R still works. Behind as many levels as possible, LVN first (pp.305–308)
TargetHVN in the trade’s direction = the area. Its VPOC = the price. Naked VPOCs. No profile to the left → earlier highs/lows, climaxes (pp.308–311)
ManageAt the first relevant level (the range edge, the VPOC): take a partial or tighten (pp.245, 284)

On daily stock charts

  • Profiles: a fixed range on the structure, or weekly/composite. Use completed profiles (pp.290–291).
  • Place stop orders after the close. A gap through them fills at the open: a triggered stop is a market order (SEC).
  • Size: shares = (account × 1%) ÷ |entry − stop|.
  • Price left without you? Don’t chase. Your read was right. Plan the next zone (pp.311–313).
