Spring or MSOW? After the break

Price has closed under the Ice. Is it a Spring or an MSOW? You can’t tell on the day of the break. Here is what to watch for after it, and what a holder does at each step.

1 · Weigh the traces in order (2.0 pp.72–81)

TraceStrengthWeakness
Phase A testEnds at the top, or above the BCEnds at the bottom, or below the AR
AR (Classic pp.113–114)Slow, no volume peakDeep and heavy
Phase B testAt the top, and then price holdsAt the top, then a visit to the lows
Volume through the rangeShrinks (Classic p.76)Stays high, heavier on the falls
Phase C shakeoutSpring + re-entryUTAD + back in

The shakeout is “the dominant event”, and the latest information counts most (2.0 p.78). Earlier traces are smaller pieces. Score each one as it looked that day, and call a trace “no clear sign” when neither side is pressing.

2 · Spring or MSOW?

WatchSpringMSOW
The break itselfLight to moderate volume (Spring #2, #3)Heavy, wide, often a gap after a report
The rally backCloses back above the IceReaches it, closes under (LPSY)
The pullback afterNarrower, lighter, holds over the break’s lowA new low

The first row only hints. The second row decides. A Spring #1 (heavy break) or #2 needs a test before you buy; a #3 (light) can be bought directly (Classic pp.144–149).

3 · The holder’s rule, both halves (mine)

WhenDo
First close under the IceOut (Lesson 17)
Rally back closes under itStay out. It’s an LPSY
Rally back closes above itPotential Spring. Watch for the test
Quiet test holds over the break’s lowBuy back. Stop under the Spring low
Price closes back under the Spring lowOut. The shakeout failed
  • Stop: under the Spring low. It’s often wide, so the position is small.
  • Target: the other end of the range, the shakeout’s “minimum objective” (2.0 p.78). Take part at the BC high on the way.
  • Check the index. A Spring on the day the whole market flushes is common. A break while the index rises is the stock’s own.
